# Article 10: website speed and conversion rates

Checked: 11 October 2026. Author: Konstantinos Kyprianou. [Complete draft](../blog-drafts/website-speed-conversion-rates.md).

## Purpose and editorial decisions

Owns business-outcome evidence, the definition and interpretation of conversion rates, qualified enquiries, contribution estimates and evaluation of a performance change. Article 9 owns initial impressions, the three-second heuristic and metric thresholds. Articles 21 and 22 will own optimization implementation and performance's SEO role. Their material is not reproduced here.

The planner asks for proof that a one-second improvement directly lifts conversions and calls the topic a million-dollar metric. There is no universal one-second coefficient or established monetary outcome for this business. The draft makes the business case through bounded published evidence and clearly identified illustrative arithmetic.

Voice follows the established [Konstantinos standard](konstantinos-voice-and-search.md): one reader, concrete business consequences, candid first-person judgment and a practical action. The campaign opening is hypothetical. No client project, anecdote, performance score, studio conversion rate or income result is invented.

The historical cases are labelled with their dates. Research checked today does not mean their underlying observations took place in 2026. Vendor-published case studies are reported evidence, not independently replicated Konaverse findings.

## Illustrative calculations

The comparison uses 10,000 sessions in each column, with 250 and 300 converting sessions. The rates are 2.5% and 3.0%; the change is 0.5 percentage points or 20% relative. No significance or causal conclusion is assigned to this hypothetical table.

The later example explicitly assumes one distinct genuine enquiry per additional converting session, a 40% qualification rate and 25% win rate: 50 additional enquiries × 0.40 = 20 qualified opportunities; 20 × 0.25 = five estimated projects. These are illustrative assumptions, not studio benchmarks. No project-price increment, average deal amount or guaranteed ROI is invented.

Contribution is defined after direct delivery costs. Capacity, sales delay, consistent measurement and comparable reporting periods are part of the interpretation. A business's observed win rate does not establish that every additional lead will have the same value.

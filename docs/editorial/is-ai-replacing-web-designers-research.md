# Article 8: AI and the value of professional web design

Checked: 10 October 2026. Author: Konstantinos Kyprianou. [Draft](../blog-drafts/is-ai-replacing-web-designers.md).

## Distinct editorial purpose

Owns changing design tasks, buyer expectations, creative direction and ways to assess a premium concept. Article 7 owns AI website-building capability and launch verification. Article 11 will cover the visual details of luxury websites. This is not a tool ranking, labor-market prediction or claim that every premium business needs an agency.

The supplied hook claims AI normalizes mediocrity and human custom work is automatically a luxury differentiator. The draft treats specificity, coherence and verification as assessable qualities. Both AI-assisted and wholly human work can be strong or generic. Human involvement, manual labor and custom code are not automatic evidence of originality.

ILO research is broader labor-market exposure analysis. It is not a Cyprus web-design employment census, a count of layoffs, or proof that no designers will be replaced. No percentages are reproduced and no outcome is forecast for a specific occupation or company.

The hotel example is explicitly hypothetical. Brand direction, design coherence and the three review recommendations are the author's editorial position. They are not psychological findings attributed to ILO or W3C, and there is no measured uplift in bookings or perceived luxury.

The draft does not claim Konaverse uses a specific AI product for clients. Recommendations concern how to evaluate any agency's delivery, including AI-assisted work. Pricing consequences are deliberately conditional; no invented productivity, margin or discount figures appear.

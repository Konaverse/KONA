## The voice to carry forward

The recurring pattern is a person speaking to another person about a problem they recognize. Konstantinos then explains his reasoning, offers a concrete action and connects it to something that matters to the reader. His authority comes through conviction, usefulness and personal accountability.

For Konaverse, write as an experienced technical founder guiding a business owner. Keep the directness, warmth and ambition of the older work. Use polished grammar and evidence appropriate to the subject. The samples inform the voice; they do not establish current client experiences or technical results.

- **Address one reader.** Use “you” and “your team.” Describe what the reader needs to do on an ordinary working day.
- **Give the author a position.** Use “I” for judgment and standards, and “we” for verified studio facts. First-person opinions are appropriate in a ghostwritten draft; invented memories, conversations and client outcomes are not.
- **Explain through consequences.** Show how an awkward dashboard leaves an article unpublished, or how an undefined editing scope creates another development request.
- **Use concrete examples.** A biography reused across three pages explains structured content more clearly than a paragraph of terminology. Keep hypothetical examples identifiable as such.
- **Vary the rhythm.** Mostly connected paragraphs, with an occasional short sentence at a moment that deserves emphasis. Avoid turning every paragraph into a one-line maxim.
- **Let questions do useful work.** Use them to guide a decision or acknowledge a reader's concern. Avoid a chain of rhetorical questions followed by canned answers.
- **Give the reader an action.** End with something they can inspect, test or ask for. Let the service link follow from that action.
- **Keep warmth in the ending.** “Until next time.” appears across the samples and has been carried through Articles 2–12. Keep it unless the owner changes that preference.

Authoritative means precise about the recommendation, its reasons and its boundaries. Qualify an outcome when implementation affects it. Avoid inflated language such as “infinite freedom,” “flawless,” “eliminates security risks forever” or “Google loves this.”

## Style calibration after Articles 2–12

Use [Article 10](../blog-drafts/website-speed-conversion-rates.md), [Article 11](../blog-drafts/how-to-make-a-website-look-luxury.md) and [Article 12](../blog-drafts/luxury-vs-premium-brand-positioning.md) as recent applications: a recognizable business situation, a clear founder judgment, sources with their limits and an action the reader can take. They cover different purposes and should not become interchangeable templates.

Start with an accurate answer, then connect it to a real decision. Vary the following opening: a publishing obstacle, proposal uncertainty, a booking journey or a question about what customers value. Keep paragraphs connected and alternate sentence lengths naturally. Avoid strings of one-line slogans, repeated “I recommend” constructions, generic trend introductions and stock contrast formulas.

Polish the English without removing warmth. Explain technical terms where the business reader needs them. Use ordinary details to make an idea recognizable, and label hypothetical situations clearly. Add an aside only when it belongs to the subject; do not manufacture personal stories to imitate the older samples. Human style is not an AI-detector score or a reason to introduce errors.

Each article needs its own contribution: a useful handover test, proposal comparison, measurement method or brief. A table or checklist earns its place by helping the decision. It does not need to appear in every article. Let the service link follow naturally from the final action.

## The repeatable article standard

Start with the reader's main question and a clear answer. Introduce the business consequence early. Explain the mechanism in plain language, then offer the comparisons, limits and decision steps the reader needs. Include a distinctive judgment or practical method that adds something beyond vendor definitions.

Use the target query where it naturally describes the subject. Use related concepts to complete the explanation, without a keyword-density target or a heading for every query variation. Each article should have a distinct purpose within the series.

Link a factual assertion to the primary source that supports it. Vendor documentation verifies a capability; vendor promotional language does not prove business outcomes. Add numerical claims only when the original evidence and its context are relevant. Never invent rankings, conversion gains or project results to make an article look authoritative.

Give each draft a clear H1, a useful proposed search title, an accurate description, a stable slug and a named author. Google can rewrite titles and snippets, and character counts are editorial checks rather than ranking thresholds.

## Research record and claim review

Create a separate research note for each new article under `docs/editorial/<slug>-research.md`, following the records for Articles 3–12. Article 2's evidence remains in this guide. Record the article's unique purpose, sources, supported findings, limitations, access method, hypothetical examples and publication considerations.

Keep research-check dates separate from source publication and observation dates. Distinguish full text or relevant PDF sections from abstracts and search extracts. Record access limits rather than claiming to have read an inaccessible paper. Prefer primary evidence appropriate to the claim; a company's own documentation proves its described capability or stated position, not independently measured business outcomes.

Distinguish experiments from observations and recommendations from validated methods. For numerical claims, keep populations, denominators and percentage-point changes clear. Check every hypothetical calculation. For repository examples, confirm the active v4 implementation and do not imply that local work is deployed or that headless profiles establish physical-device smoothness.

Correct unsupported planner hooks in the draft and explain the correction in the research note and planner draft entry. This has already removed promises about complete editing control, security immunity, framework-determined rankings, universal AI incapability and a fixed conversion lift per second. No keyword volumes, rankings, enquiries or AI citations were measured in this drafting work.

Before completing a draft, review claims and sources, the first-person voice, examples, overlap with neighboring articles, metadata, one H1, encoding and reference paths. Check that the related service exists and that article links have a publication plan. Update the [planner](../content-planner-three-months.md) and [writing handoff](../blog-writing-handoff.md) together. These checks support editorial quality; no fixed article length or guaranteed SEO/GEO outcome follows from passing them.

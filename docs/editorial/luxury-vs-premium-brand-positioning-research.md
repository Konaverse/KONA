# Article 12: luxury versus premium brand positioning

Checked: 11 October 2026. Author: Konstantinos Kyprianou. [Complete draft](../blog-drafts/luxury-vs-premium-brand-positioning.md).

## Purpose and editorial decisions

Owns the positioning decision behind the visual direction: the customer's reasons to choose, evidence supporting the offer, operational delivery and the website's content priorities. Article 11 owns typography, imagery, spacing and motion; it is linked rather than repeated in detail. Later articles own scroll storytelling, redesign planning and implementation.

The supplied planner asks which is better, premium or luxury, and proposes alignment with a tier to attract ultra-wealthy clients. The draft treats the choice as a fit between the offer, audience and business. It does not promise that luxury styling or a positioning label attracts a particular wealth group. Premium is not presented as a deficient stage of luxury. Quality, emotion, craftsmanship, service and history can overlap.

The definitions and comparison table are a working editorial lens for a website brief. They are not a universal taxonomy or a validated brand classification instrument. The four decision questions and fill-in positioning brief are original editorial recommendations, with no numerical score or certification attached.

Voice follows the [Konstantinos standard](konstantinos-voice-and-search.md): direct address, first-person standards, practical consequences and a concrete next step. The two furniture workshops are explicitly hypothetical and have different offers. No client anecdote, brand transformation, project outcome, spending uplift or income threshold is invented. Konaverse itself is not assigned an unsupported luxury-market classification.

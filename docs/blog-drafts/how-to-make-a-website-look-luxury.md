# How to Make a Website Look Luxury: 7 Design Choices

*By Konstantinos Kyprianou*

To make a website look luxury, give its typography, imagery, spacing, colour and interactions a clear, consistent character. Make important content easy to notice, show details that reveal the quality of your work, and carry the same care through mobile pages and enquiries. The premium impression comes from those decisions working together.

My first question is what you want someone to appreciate about your business.

For an architecture studio, it could be the way a home brings light into an ordinary working day. For a furniture maker, it might be the fit of a joint or the finish of a surface. Those are things a website can help someone examine.

Once you know what deserves attention, the design has a purpose. You can choose an image, a type treatment and a pace that help the visitor understand it.

I want your website to show why you take pride in the business. Those specific qualities give us a foundation for its visual character.

## 1. Choose what the visitor should notice first

A visual anchor is the element that gives a page its focus: a photograph, a headline, a product or a significant detail. Choose it according to the message the page needs to communicate.

Suppose you run an architecture studio. A photograph of a completed home could lead the page, supported by a clear description of your practice and a route to selected projects. The visitor can appreciate the building and understand who is responsible for it.

The headline, image and next step need a visible order. You can create that through scale, placement, contrast and the space around them. When several elements demand equal attention, deciding what to look at becomes work for the visitor.

A [2012 study by Tuch and colleagues](https://research.google/pubs/the-role-of-visual-complexity-and-prototypicality-regarding-first-impression-of-websites-working-towards-understanding-aesthetic-judgments/) found that visual complexity and how closely a website resembled its category affected aesthetic judgments of screenshots. Lower complexity and greater category familiarity were rated more appealing in those experiments. This measured visual appeal, rather than luxury status or purchases.

My practical recommendation is to make the business and its main action recognizable while giving the presentation its own character. Someone should be able to admire the work and find the project information without learning an unfamiliar navigation system.

## 2. Use space to establish relationships

Negative space is the unoccupied area around content. Its job is to help you see what belongs together and what deserves a separate moment of attention.

Keep a project caption close enough to its photograph that the connection is obvious. Give the next project a more generous separation. Align the heading, description and action so they read as a considered group.

The gaps should help explain the page.

An expansive composition can give a photograph room to be appreciated. A tighter passage can bring related specifications together. The change in density helps the page express which material you should examine and which you should read as a group.

Start with consistent margins and a clear alignment system. Then make deliberate exceptions: perhaps a wide photograph interrupts a narrower text column, while its caption returns to the established edge. The surrounding order makes the change feel intentional.

Review the actual content in that layout. A long service name, an extra paragraph or a project with several images should still feel like part of the same website.

## 3. Build typography around the words you need to publish

Typography gives the website a voice before someone finishes reading the sentence. Its character comes from the typeface, size, weight, spacing and arrangement of the words.

Choose those together. A distinctive headline needs body text that is comfortable to read, captions that remain legible and buttons whose labels are clear.

A serif face may suit one brand; a precise sans serif may suit another. Bespoke typography can mean a carefully developed treatment of an existing family. A custom font is a separate choice whose cost and usefulness should follow the brief.

Our Konaverse design system uses Manrope as its type family. Size, weight and composition give different parts of the page their character. Explore those relationships when you review a proposed type system.

Test proposed typography with your own material. Use the longest project name, a normal service paragraph and the labels in your enquiry form. If you publish in Greek and English, review both scripts. A beautiful sample word tells you very little about a full page in either language.

A [2025 study in Tourism Management](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S026151772400178X) examined font and corner curvature on mock luxury hotel pages. It found that readability and the pleasure of the browsing experience contributed to perceived luxury in that experiment. Those findings belong to a particular hospitality test; they are a useful reason to assess how your actual words feel and read within the proposed design.

## 4. Show the details that make your work worth examining

Give every important image a reason to be on the page. A visitor should learn something about the product, place or service by looking at it.

A furniture maker might show the whole object, then its joinery and material finish. An architecture studio might move from the building in its setting to a room and then a detail that explains the design. The sequence gives the viewer something specific to appreciate.

Agree on the photographic direction before collecting assets: lighting, viewpoints, colour treatment and the details each image needs to reveal. Existing photography can work well when the selection has a clear purpose and the images belong together.

Add useful context. Name the project, explain your role and say whether the image represents completed work or a proposal. That helps someone judge what they are seeing.

The crop needs attention too. A wide image can look convincing on a laptop while making its subject tiny on a phone. [MDN's responsive-image guidance](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/HTML/Guides/Responsive_images) distinguishes choosing an appropriate image resolution from changing the composition for different layouts. These are separate decisions: the file must suit the display, and the crop must preserve what matters.

Ask to review the images at their actual display sizes. Keep sufficient detail, check compression quality and choose mobile compositions deliberately. The aim is to deliver the image efficiently while keeping its purpose intact.

## 5. Give colour a role in the brand

A useful palette starts with your identity and the material you have to show. It should help the website feel like your business.

For example, a vivid colour from a craft brand's packaging could give its website a recognizable accent. A studio working with natural materials might draw on the tones in its photography. A monochrome direction can give differences in light, texture and scale more room to work.

These are possible directions to explore, rather than rules assigning luxury to a particular colour.

Define how the palette behaves: which colour carries the main text, which surfaces contain information, and how links and actions stand out. Apply those roles consistently through the site.

Keep the text comfortable to read. [WCAG 2.2's minimum text-contrast criterion](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/contrast-minimum.html) specifies 4.5:1 for normal text and 3:1 for qualifying large text, with defined exceptions. These are contrast ratios between text and its background.

This matters especially when a light caption sits over a photograph. Its background changes with the image and crop. Check the actual composition and adjust the text placement or treatment so the words remain readable.

## 6. Give motion a purpose and a consistent pace

Motion can help explain a relationship, reveal a detail or show what changed after someone acted. Decide what it contributes before choosing the effect.

A controlled image reveal might direct attention to a material detail. A menu transition can show where navigation has opened. A gallery can help someone move through a project in a considered order.

Let those movements share a character. Their speed, direction and timing should make sense together, just as the type and imagery do. The visitor also needs to be able to move on when they are ready.

Review repeat visits as well as the first arrival. Follow a project link, return to the previous page and open the contact step. The choreography needs to support those ordinary movements through the website.

Respect reduced-motion preferences and keep the content available when effects are disabled. [W3C's guidance on animation from interactions](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/animation-from-interactions.html) explains allowing people to disable nonessential motion triggered by interaction. That is a useful standard to build into the design brief.

Check the experience on a phone too. If the effect makes a page difficult to navigate or delays an action, review its design and delivery together. [The speed and conversion article](/blog/website-speed-conversion-rates) explains how to connect those delays to business outcomes.

## 7. Carry the same care through mobile and enquiries

The quality of the presentation needs to continue after the opening screen. A service description, project page and contact form all contribute to the experience of your business.

On a phone, revisit the composition. Adjust headline wrapping, image framing and spacing for the available room. Preserve the details someone needs to understand the offer and choose their next step.

Check enlarged text as well. [W3C's reflow guidance](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/reflow.html) addresses keeping information and functionality available as content fits a narrower viewing area. Review whether the words can be read comfortably without awkward horizontal movement.

Then complete an enquiry. The labels, instructions, error messages and confirmation should receive the same attention as the project photography. A helpful confirmation tells the visitor what has happened and what to expect next.

For me, that continuity is part of the craft. The care you put into presenting the business should also be present when someone asks to work with you.

## Turn "make it luxury" into a useful design brief

Consider a hypothetical architecture studio whose strength is thoughtful residential work in Cyprus. Here is how it could make the direction concrete:

| Part of the page | A specific decision for this example |
| --- | --- |
| Opening description | State "Residential architecture in Cyprus" so the visitor understands the practice. |
| Image sequence | Lead with a completed home in its setting, then show an interior and a relevant construction detail. |
| Project information | Identify the location, scope, project status and the studio's role, with captions close to the images. |
| Next step | Make selected projects easy to browse and provide a clearly labelled route to discuss a new home. |

The designer can now explain how the typography, space, colour and motion support that material. You can assess the proposal against a business you recognize.

Before approving a direction, review three real page types together: the homepage, a service or project page, and the contact page. I recommend three checks:

1. **Describe the character.** Identify the choices that make the site feel like your business. Connect them to your work, audience and identity.
2. **Follow the attention.** Explain what should be noticed first on each page, what supports it and where the visitor can go next.
3. **Complete the visit.** Read on a phone, enlarge the text and make a test enquiry. Review whether the presentation and interactions maintain the same care.

## Make the quality of your business easier to see

A premium impression is built through a series of decisions: the right image, the right words, a considered composition and an experience that carries them through.

Choose the detail you want a visitor to appreciate about your business. Bring it to the brief, along with the content and evidence that can show it convincingly.

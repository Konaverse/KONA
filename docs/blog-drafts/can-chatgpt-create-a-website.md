# Can ChatGPT Create a Website? What to Check Before You Launch

*By Konstantinos Kyprianou*

Yes. ChatGPT can help create, edit and, through supported workflows, host a website. AI tools can produce layouts, code, content and working interactions. Whether the result is ready for your business depends on the brief, the connected services, testing and ongoing ownership. A working preview establishes that something has been built; it still needs to be assessed against what you need.

You describe your business, wait for the result and suddenly there is a website on the screen. The colours look right. The menu opens. The page even moves when you scroll.

That is useful progress. I understand why it makes the traditional website process look unnecessarily complicated.

My first check is the part that matters most to the business. If the site is supposed to generate enquiries, submit one and follow it all the way to the person who needs to receive it.

That small test changes the conversation. You begin assessing the website as something you will use.

## What ChatGPT can do for a website today

As of October 2026, [OpenAI's Sites documentation](https://learn.chatgpt.com/docs/sites) describes a ChatGPT workflow for creating, hosting, refining and sharing websites and web apps. Availability and limits depend on the account and supported workflow.

That means the answer has moved beyond "it can write some HTML." A suitable workflow can give you a hosted experience. Other workflows produce code that you deploy through a separate service.

AI can also help explore layouts, modify existing components, draft copy and investigate errors. With appropriate tools and access, coding agents can run checks and work through more substantial development tasks.

The result can go well beyond a basic static page. Its usefulness depends on the supplied context, the tools and the way the work is directed and verified.

The useful distinction is between a capability and a completed requirement. A tool that can build a form has not necessarily connected your form to the correct inbox, protected the endpoint and tested its failure behavior.

## A good brief matters more when generation is fast

"Build me a premium website" gives the tool a direction, but leaves most business decisions open.

Which services should appear first? Which claims can you substantiate? What information does a buyer need before making contact? Who will update the pages? Should an enquiry book a call or provide project details?

If you do not supply those answers, the tool may fill the gaps with plausible choices. Those choices may fit your business, or they may simply look reasonable.

For illustration, a consultancy might need visitors to understand that its assessment service and implementation service are separate engagements. A polished homepage that blends them together can create confusion even if its code works perfectly.

Give the tool the approved service descriptions, audience, relevant evidence and intended next step. Also define what it should do when information is missing. Asking it to identify gaps is more useful than allowing it to invent case studies or testimonials.

Fast generation makes the decisions easier to explore. You still need to know which decisions are right.

## Check the whole enquiry journey

Suppose your generated website includes a contact form. A success message alone does not establish that the enquiry reached your business.

Use a realistic test submission and verify where it arrives, what information is included and what the visitor is told. Try the common failure cases too: an invalid email address, a missing required field and a service that is temporarily unavailable.

If the website accepts uploads or connects to a CRM, those behaviors need their own review. Decide who can see the information and how the integration will be maintained.

You can ask AI to help test these paths. The evidence should come from the actual behavior of the system. "The form is implemented" is a description of work, not confirmation that the business received the message.

For a business website, this is a more useful place to spend attention than the tenth revision of a decorative animation.

## Verify the content before it represents your company

Generated copy can sound certain even when the brief did not provide the facts.

Read every claim about services, qualifications, project results, locations and clients. Trace those claims to material you approved. Replace invented proof with real evidence or remove it.

The same applies to images. A compelling visual may still be unsuitable if it implies that an invented building, product or project belongs to your business.

I would also look at whether the page helps someone make a decision. A paragraph can be grammatically polished and still fail to explain what you do, whom you serve or how an engagement works.

Good copy needs both accuracy and usefulness. AI can assist with both, but the website owner has to establish the underlying facts.

## Review accessibility, mobile use and performance

Open the website on a small phone with real content. Check the navigation, headings, image crops and primary action. Enlarge the text. Try completing the main task with a keyboard.

Automated accessibility checks can help identify issues, but [W3C explains that tools alone cannot determine whether a website meets accessibility standards](https://www.w3.org/WAI/test-evaluate/). A generated result deserves the same evaluation as other development work.

For performance, test the pages people will visit, including service and contact pages. Look beyond the first screenshot: does the menu respond, does a layout shift move the button, and is the booking experience ready when someone opens it?

Ask for measured evidence under stated conditions. A fast preview on a developer's machine does not describe every visitor's experience.

You do not need to remove personality from the website to take this seriously. You need to understand what its images, code and interactions ask the device to do.

## Confirm the search basics on the finished pages

A page that looks good in a preview can still have problems with its URLs, page information or indexability.

Check that each important page has a stable address, accurate title, readable main content and appropriate internal links. Verify that the public release is accessible to search engines and that staging restrictions have been handled deliberately.

The framework can provide useful tools, but it does not decide whether the content answers a searcher's needs. [Google's helpful-content guidance](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/creating-helpful-content) emphasizes useful, reliable content created for people.

If the website uses React or Next.js, [the framework SEO comparison](/blog/nextjs-vs-react-seo) explains what to inspect in the implementation. Asking an AI tool to "make it SEO optimized" still needs a concrete definition and a review of the result.

## Is it legal to ask AI to create a website?

You can ask AI to help create a website under the tool's applicable terms. That permission does not clear every image, font, piece of code or business claim used in the finished site.

OpenAI's [Europe Terms of Use](https://openai.com/policies/eu-terms-of-use/) and [Services Agreement](https://openai.com/policies/services-agreement/) address ownership of output between the user or customer and OpenAI, subject to applicable law. The agreement relevant to your account matters. Contractual ownership does not by itself establish exclusive copyright or permission to use third-party material.

For a Cyprus business, European copyright rules are relevant. [Article 1 of the EU computer-program directive](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=CELEX:32009L0024) sets an originality requirement for software protection. It does not, by itself, settle the copyright status of a particular AI-assisted website.

Check licenses for reused code, fonts and images, and the rights to anything you supply. A website that collects personal information also needs appropriate handling of that information. Specific copyright or data-protection questions depend on the materials, implementation and applicable law.

The practical action is to document the assets and dependencies you use. An AI-generated answer saying "commercial use is fine" is not a substitute for their actual terms.

## Decide who will maintain the result

Before adopting the website, establish who controls the domain, hosting, content and connected accounts. Know how updates are made and how the project can be recovered if something fails.

If a workflow generates a local code project, check that someone can build, deploy and maintain it. If it hosts the website for you, understand the platform's features, access arrangements and ongoing limits.

This is an ordinary ownership question, and it applies to agency-built websites too. The source of the code does not remove the need for a working handover.

I would be comfortable with an AI-assisted build when the requirements and responsibilities are clear and the result has been verified. I would hesitate over an impressive demonstration that nobody knows how to operate.

## When AI is enough, and when expert help is useful

AI may be enough for a focused personal site, a prototype or a straightforward business page when you can provide the content, verify the behavior and support the result.

Expert help becomes more useful as the consequences of mistakes increase: a complex offer, important integrations, existing search traffic, sensitive data or a brand experience that needs careful direction.

Those experts can use AI too. Their value should be visible in the decisions, validation and support they provide. For the design side of that discussion, read [whether AI is replacing web designers](/blog/is-ai-replacing-web-designers).

Before launch, follow one real visitor journey from arrival to the intended outcome. Review the content, accessibility, search setup and ownership around it. If you need help completing that work, it belongs in the scope of a [web development project](/services/web-development).

I care much more about whether the website serves your business reliably than how its first version was produced.

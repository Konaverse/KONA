# Is AI Replacing Web Designers? What Premium Brands Still Need

*By Konstantinos Kyprianou*

AI can automate parts of web design and development, and it is changing what clients need to pay people to do. That does not establish that the whole profession is disappearing. For a premium brand, the valuable work includes deciding what the website should communicate, directing the experience, checking how people use it and taking responsibility for the result. AI can support that work; the project still needs clear decisions and standards.

If you can generate ten website concepts in an afternoon, paying someone simply to produce the first layout becomes harder to justify.

I think that is a fair challenge.

It should push designers and agencies to explain their value more clearly. A business owner is entitled to ask what expertise contributes when the production tools are becoming more capable.

The answer needs to show up in the website. It might be a clearer explanation of a complex service, a more convincing use of evidence, or an experience that holds together when someone actually tries to make an enquiry.

Having a person involved does not automatically make the work original. Giving a tool a prompt does not automatically make the result generic. We need to assess the choices and the outcome.

## AI is changing tasks, and some roles will change with them

AI can help produce layouts, develop interfaces, explore imagery and revise copy. It can also assist with analysis and testing when the relevant information and tools are available.

Those capabilities affect how much production work a project requires and which skills a team needs. It would be unrealistic to pretend that every role will remain exactly as it was.

At the same time, task exposure and observed job replacement are different things. In its [September 2025 explanation of generative AI and work](https://www.ilo.org/resource/article/generative-ai-work-what-it-means-jobs-europe-and-beyond), the International Labour Organization discusses growing exposure in roles including web and multimedia development, while explaining that exposure does not mean an entire occupation is immediately automated.

That research describes broader labor-market exposure. It does not tell us how many Cyprus web designers have lost work or predict the outcome for an individual studio.

My view is that designers should develop their ability to direct and assess work, including work made with AI. A promise that "humans will always be needed" is much less useful than showing why a particular project needs particular expertise.

## Premium design begins with understanding the business

Consider a hypothetical boutique hotel. It offers private stays in a quiet location, with a small number of rooms and unusually attentive service.

A tool can generate a visually impressive hotel website. So can a designer. Either can miss the point by making the property look like a large resort with a busy entertainment programme.

The useful work begins with discovering what makes the stay desirable, what kind of guest it suits and what that guest needs to know before booking. Real photographs, room information, the booking process and the tone of the copy all need to support that understanding.

This is a good brief for AI-assisted exploration. It is also a good brief for a designer. The quality of the direction matters to both.

For a premium business, I would want the design team to explain the connection between the brand and its proposed experience. "It looks luxurious" leaves too much unanswered. The website should help a visitor understand the particular value of this business.

## Generating options is useful; choosing among them is work

More concepts can help you discover a direction faster. They can also make a project drift if nobody has agreed what makes one option better than another.

One concept may be striking but hide the offer. Another may be clear but resemble every other business in the category. A third may fit the brand but rely on imagery that the company cannot supply.

Those trade-offs need discussion before you spend time refining the details.

I would evaluate concepts against a short brief: the audience, the main message, the evidence available and the action the visitor should take. Then I would ask the team to explain why the selected direction serves that brief.

AI can help compare alternatives when given useful criteria. A design team still needs to agree which criteria matter and verify whether the chosen result meets them. Producing more variations is not a substitute for making a decision.

## A coherent website needs more than a strong homepage

A brand experience continues across service pages, forms, case studies and mobile navigation. The most dramatic screen may be a small part of the visit.

Imagine the hotel website uses an atmospheric homepage, but its room pages have different typography, unclear availability links and image crops that hide the details guests want to see. The initial concept has not been carried through the booking journey.

A useful design system helps the team make consistent choices as content grows. It defines how typography, spacing, imagery and components work across different situations.

AI can help implement and extend that system. It needs access to the relevant decisions and a way to check the result. A human team benefits from the same discipline.

For me, a premium project deserves attention in its ordinary states: a long heading, a missing optional image, a form error and a smaller screen. These are places where the business experience can lose the care shown in the presentation.

## A designer should help verify the experience

A designer's value includes being able to assess the experience with methods appropriate to the project. That can involve reviewing content, observing someone complete a task, evaluating accessibility and working with developers on performance.

Tools help, but their output still needs interpretation. [W3C's accessibility guidance](https://www.w3.org/WAI/test-evaluate/) explains the need for knowledgeable evaluation alongside automated tools.

A navigation menu might pass an automated check and still be confusing. A visual effect might work in a screenshot while making a mobile interaction awkward. A contact page might be readable but ask for information the buyer is not ready to provide.

These are questions the team can investigate. AI can assist with finding issues, generating test cases or exploring alternatives. Claims about usability should be supported by inspecting the relevant behavior and, where appropriate, testing with people.

I want a designer to be curious about those results. Defending the first concept regardless of what the evidence shows is a poor use of expertise.

## What you should expect from an AI-assisted agency

You do not need the agency to perform every repetitive task manually. You do need it to explain the scope, apply suitable review and own its delivery responsibilities.

Ask how approved content and design decisions reach the build, how generated material is checked, and how the team verifies the finished pages. If the project uses third-party assets or dependencies, their terms and maintenance requirements still matter.

At Konaverse, creative direction and technical architecture are distinct responsibilities. Nabil Al Jbawi is the studio's creative director; I am its technical architect. That division makes the collaboration concrete: a design decision has to be understood in both its visual purpose and its behavior on the website.

It is reasonable to ask any agency who performs those roles, whether or not it uses AI.

The [article on ChatGPT-built websites](/blog/can-chatgpt-create-a-website) covers launch verification in more detail. For design procurement, I would focus on who sets the direction, who reviews the work and what evidence informs their decisions.

## Is professional web design still worth paying for?

Yes, when the expertise addresses a need your business cannot meet adequately on its own. That need should be identifiable in the project.

If you need a simple page, have a clear brief and can verify an AI-generated result, a full design engagement may be unnecessary. A focused review might be enough.

If your business has a difficult offer to explain, a distinctive brand position or a complicated journey from interest to enquiry, experienced design work may have more value. The same is true when several stakeholders need a coherent system they can continue to use.

The fee should follow the work and responsibility involved. AI-assisted production may change that work, but it does not establish a universal discount or a reason to charge more. Look at the actual deliverables and the quality of the decisions.

[The web agency pricing guide](/blog/web-design-agency-pricing) gives you a way to compare those scopes. You should be able to understand what the team contributes beyond producing screens.

## Three ways to assess a premium website concept

Before approving a direction, I would use these three reviews:

1. **Explain the business fit.** Ask the team to connect the structure, imagery and tone to your audience and offer. The explanation should be specific enough to distinguish your business from another one.
2. **Follow the main visitor task.** Review the path from a relevant entry page to an enquiry or booking, including mobile use and errors. Identify what is verified and what remains an assumption.
3. **Change the content.** Try a longer service name, another project and a new team profile. Check whether the design system accommodates the material your business will actually publish.

Use these reviews to make the discussion concrete. You can connect the concept to your business, inspect its behavior and see how it handles the content you will publish.

## Pay for thoughtful direction and a result you can use

AI has made parts of website production more accessible. I welcome the pressure that puts on us to do useful work and explain it clearly.

For your brand, the decision is whether the finished experience communicates the right things, works for the people using it and has a team accountable for its delivery.

If those decisions need careful attention, they belong in a [professional web design engagement](/services/web-design). The standard should stay high as the tools improve.

Until next time.

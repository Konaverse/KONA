# How Much Should You Pay a Professional Web Design Agency?

*By Konstantinos Kyprianou*

The right web agency budget depends on what you need delivered: design, development, content, editing tools, integrations and support. At Konaverse, our starting budgets include €1,200 for a one-page website, €2,500 for design and development of a standard website with five main pages, and €6,000 for a 3D website. These are our starting prices, not industry averages or final quotes for every project.

Receiving a website quote should make the project clearer. Sometimes it does the opposite.

One agency sends a single number. Another sends twelve pages of terminology. Both promise a professional website, and you are left trying to decide whether the difference in price represents different work or different confidence.

Start by asking each team to describe the same finished result. You can assess a price much more fairly when you know what that price includes.

Here is the budget discussion I would want you to have before committing.

## Konaverse's starting website budgets

The following prices reflect the studio's pricing recorded in October 2026. Your project quote defines the final scope and commercial terms.

| Project or service | Starting budget | How to read it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| One-page website | From €1,200 | A focused website; required content and functionality still need scoping |
| Web design on its own | From €1,800 | Design work, with development quoted separately if required |
| Web development on its own | From €1,800 | Development work, with the supplied design and requirements assessed first |
| Design and development together | From €2,500 | Minimum for a standard website with five main pages |
| Website redesign | From €2,500 | Existing content, URLs and migration requirements affect scope |
| 3D website | From €6,000 | The experience, assets, interactions and device requirements affect scope |

Design-only and development-only are separate engagement types. Adding their starting figures together is not how the combined project's starting price is calculated.

Equally, five pages do not define everything a website does. A five-page brochure site and a five-page site with multilingual publishing, booking and custom integrations have different requirements.

The written quote should confirm the pages, features, supplied materials, revisions, VAT treatment and third-party charges. A starting price is useful context. Those details establish what you are buying.

## What a professional agency fee should cover

A proposal should connect the fee to deliverables and responsibilities you can recognize. Depending on the engagement, those may include understanding the business, organizing content, designing the experience, developing it and preparing it for launch.

Look for evidence of the work behind broad labels.

If the proposal includes strategy, which decisions will be made? If it includes responsive design, which page types and interactions are covered? If it includes CMS training, what will your team learn to publish?

Testing also needs a defined scope. [W3C's accessibility planning guidance](https://www.w3.org/WAI/planning-and-managing/plan/) includes budgeting for reviews, training, audits and user testing according to the work required. A proposal should explain the accessibility work included rather than treating it as an unexplained badge.

I would expect the agency to tell you who reviews the design, who builds it and who handles issues during delivery. You do not need a large team for every project. You need the relevant work to have an owner.

## What moves a website above its starting price?

The most useful price explanation identifies the additional work your brief creates. Here are common reasons the scope grows.

### More distinct page types

Adding ten case studies to an existing case-study template is different from designing ten new page layouts. The quote should distinguish reusable templates from the amount of content to enter.

A team page, service page and project gallery may each require different layouts and fields even when they count as only three pages.

### Content and visual assets

Approved text and usable photographs let the team work from something concrete. Copywriting, photography, image licensing or substantial content restructuring are additional deliverables when you need them.

Ask whether the agency enters your content, prepares the images and helps resolve missing material. "Client supplies content" should come with a clear description of what you need to provide and when.

### CMS scope and languages

A blog editor, a flexible service-page editor and a multilingual approval workflow involve different work. A second language also requires translated content, appropriate layouts and a clear publishing process.

Your team should know which fields and sections it can edit. For the architectural trade-offs, read [the headless CMS disadvantages guide](/blog/headless-cms-disadvantages).

### Integrations and custom behavior

An embedded booking service is different from building a custom booking system. A basic enquiry form is different from one that routes requests, uploads documents or synchronizes with a CRM.

The agency should define the behavior, the connected services and what happens when an integration fails. That definition is much more useful than a line saying "booking included."

### Motion and 3D

A small transition and a custom interactive scene require different design, asset and development work. The proposal should explain the purpose of the experience, its mobile behavior and how performance will be checked.

I prefer a simpler motion treatment when it communicates the idea just as well. Complexity should follow the brief.

### Migration and deadlines

An existing website may have articles, indexed URLs, files and integrations that need to be accounted for. When URLs change, [Google recommends mapping the old addresses to the new ones, configuring redirects and monitoring the move](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/crawling-indexing/site-move-with-url-changes).

A fixed launch deadline also affects scheduling. Tell the agency early, including the dates when your own team can provide content and approvals. A timeline based on immediate feedback will slip if the project waits two weeks for each decision.

There is no honest universal surcharge for each item above. The amount depends on the actual work. Ask for the additional deliverable and its cost in your quote.

## How to compare two agency proposals fairly

Give both agencies the same brief and compare their answers against the same requirements.

| Requirement | What to ask both agencies |
| --- | --- |
| Pages and content | Which distinct layouts are included, and who writes and enters the content? |
| Editing | Which changes can our team make after launch? |
| Functionality | What does each form, booking tool or integration actually do? |
| Design review | What approval stages and revision scope are included? |
| Validation | What will be tested, and what evidence will we receive? |
| Handover | Which accounts, source files and instructions will we receive? |
| Ongoing work | What does support include, and how are extra changes charged? |

For example, one proposal might include entering twenty existing case studies while another assumes you will do it yourself. Both can be valid offers. They assign the work differently, which changes the comparison.

Likewise, a lower fee may make sense because the project uses a narrower design scope, fewer integrations or content you already have. It may also omit something essential. Establish which situation you are looking at before judging the number.

If you want the broader investment argument, read [why custom websites cost more](/blog/why-custom-websites-are-expensive). This pricing discussion is about establishing a comparable scope.

## Include hosting and maintenance in the budget

A website continues to incur costs after the build. The domain, hosting, CMS, external services and maintenance need to be accounted for.

Konaverse's recorded hosting and maintenance baseline is €40 per month, or €90 per month with a CMS. Confirm the arrangement for your project in the written quote, including service subscriptions, usage allowances, support and any separately charged changes.

The quote should identify any included development work and list the third-party subscriptions covered.

Hosting costs can also respond to the way the website uses infrastructure. [Vercel's pricing documentation](https://vercel.com/docs/pricing), for example, describes resources billed according to usage. This is a reason to understand your hosting plan and usage assumptions, not an estimate of your future bill.

Maintenance and new development deserve separate definitions. Keeping an agreed integration working is different from adding a new service or redesigning a page. A clear agreement makes that distinction easier when the request arrives.

## Should you hire a freelancer or an agency?

A capable freelancer may be an excellent fit for a focused brief. An agency may be useful when the project needs coordinated design, content, development and delivery responsibilities.

The label does not guarantee quality in either case. Review relevant work, speak to the people responsible and ask how the project will be supported.

For a smaller budget, a clearly scoped engagement with one capable person can be more sensible than a larger team spread too thinly. For a complicated project, coordination and coverage may justify the agency model.

Choose according to the work and the people doing it. A team size on an about page is not an acceptance test for your website.

## Agree on what counts as complete

Before work begins, define the main things you will review: approved page types, populated content, working forms, the agreed editing tasks and access to the project's accounts and files.

Also agree on the payment milestones, how additional requests are handled and what support begins at launch. This protects the working relationship by giving both sides a shared expectation.

A professional agency should be comfortable explaining its scope. You should feel comfortable asking for that explanation.

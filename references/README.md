# Design references — v4 intake

Drop anything that shows me what you want here. No naming rules, no structure to follow —
a folder of screenshots with a scribbled note beats a tidy folder with no commentary.

## What helps most

**Screenshots** — full-page grabs are better than viewport crops. If the thing you like is
a *motion* (a transition, a scroll behaviour, a hover), a short screen recording or a URL +
"watch what happens at ~40% scroll" works far better than a still.

**A sentence per reference.** This is the part that actually matters. "I like this" leaves
me guessing which of the fifty things on the page you meant — the type, the spacing, the
colour, the nav, the transitions, the photography, the tone of the copy. One line naming
the *specific* thing turns a reference into a decision.

> `stripe.com` — the way the nav collapses on scroll. Not the colours.
> `ref-03.png` — this type scale. Headline-to-body ratio is what I'm after.
> `linear.app` — the page transitions. Everything else is too cold for us.

**Anti-references are just as useful.** "This is the vibe but the accent colour is
horrible" or "everyone in our space looks like this and I hate it" narrows the space
faster than another positive example.

## Also useful, if you have it

- Logo files / existing brand assets in a usable format (SVG > PNG)
- Any palette or typeface already decided
- Competitor sites — as targets *or* as things to avoid
- Real copy, if it's being rewritten alongside the design
